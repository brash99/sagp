# SAGP Executive Administration Platform

## Purpose of this document

This document introduces the SAGP Executive Administration Platform to the Society's Secretary and Assistant Secretary. It explains what the platform does, how its main workspaces fit together, and how responsibility for routine operation will pass to the incoming officers during the remainder of 2026.

The platform is more than a tool for editing a website. It is a shared administrative workspace for maintaining the Society's membership information, preparing communications, and publishing accurate information about events and calls. Its purpose is to make routine work quicker, clearer, and less dependent on any one person.

No programming knowledge should be required for ordinary use.

## The platform at a glance

The platform is organized around three workspaces.

### Membership workspace

The Membership workspace is where officers find and maintain information about SAGP members. It supports the ordinary work of reviewing a member's record, correcting or updating information, and preparing an approved change.

The intended experience is straightforward:

1. Find the member.
2. Review the information already on file.
3. Make the necessary correction or update.
4. Review a summary of the proposed change.
5. Prepare the update for approval and publication.

Keeping membership information here allows the same approved facts to support the website, membership administration, and communications without maintaining separate, conflicting lists.

### Communications workspace

The Communications workspace helps officers use the Society's approved information to reach the right people. It is intended to reduce repeated list-building and copying between systems. For example, an officer should be able to identify an appropriate group of members, review who is included, and prepare the information needed for a message or announcement.

This workspace does not replace the officers' judgment or the Society's normal email service. It helps organize reliable recipient information so that officers can concentrate on the content and purpose of a communication.

### Publishing workspace

The Publishing workspace is where officers manage information intended for the public website, including events, calls, and related announcements. It brings the Society's approved information into a reviewable publishing process instead of requiring officers to edit website files directly.

For an existing item, an officer can select it, review the current information, propose a change, and inspect the result before it is published. For a new event, the platform is moving toward a document-based process: the officer supplies a finished Word document, the system prepares a structured draft, and a person checks the draft before publication.

## One approved source for each fact

The platform is built around a simple principle: SAGP should have one approved version of each important piece of information.

We call this **canonical knowledge**. In ordinary terms, it means the Society has an authoritative record rather than several slightly different copies. A member's current email address, an event's date, or a call's deadline should be recorded once as the approved fact. The website, membership tools, and communications tools can then reuse that fact.

This approach matters because the website is only one place where Society information appears. If officers edit a webpage, a spreadsheet, and an email list separately, the copies can drift apart. The platform instead treats the approved organizational record as the source and the website as one of its outputs.

Canonical does not mean unchangeable. It means that a proposed change becomes official only after someone has reviewed and approved it. That review step protects the Society from accidental or incomplete updates.

## Current workflows

### Updating membership information

At present, an officer uses the Membership workspace to find a member, enter the necessary changes, and prepare an update request. The platform shows what will change and creates a small request file containing the proposed update.

During the transition period, that file is sent to Edward. He performs the final safety check, applies the change to the approved membership record, and publishes any resulting website update.

The request file is an internal handoff mechanism. Officers should not need to open it, understand its format, or edit it by hand.

### Updating a publication

For information already represented in the Publishing workspace, an officer selects the relevant event, call, or announcement; reviews the existing information; makes the needed correction; and prepares the proposed update. The change is reviewed before it replaces the approved version and appears on the website.

During the transition period, Edward carries out the final application and publication step. The long-term workflow will preserve the preview and confirmation safeguards while allowing the Secretary and Assistant Secretary to complete the process themselves.

### Creating an event with AI assistance

The event-creation work demonstrates the direction of the platform. Conference and event information often arrives as a finished Word document. Re-entering every title, speaker, date, affiliation, and session by hand is slow and error-prone.

The developing workflow is:

1. Begin with the finalized Word document for the event.
2. Use AI to turn the document into a structured draft.
3. Have an officer review and correct the draft.
4. Inspect a website preview.
5. Approve and publish the event.

AI is used here as a drafting assistant, not as the final decision-maker. It can save time by organizing the source document, but a person remains responsible for confirming that the result is complete and accurate. At present, parts of this process still require SysAdmin assistance; completing the officer-facing **Create Event** workflow is a near-term priority.

## The transition during 2026

The current involvement of Edward as SysAdmin is temporary. It provides a safety net while the incoming Secretary and Assistant Secretary learn the system, but it is not the intended operating model.

### Transition period

During the next few months:

- the incoming officers will learn the Membership, Communications, and Publishing workspaces through real Society tasks;
- Edward will review prepared requests, apply them, and help resolve problems;
- the team will identify unclear instructions, unnecessary steps, and missing safeguards;
- the platform will be improved in response to the officers' experience; and
- responsibility for routine review and publication will move gradually to the officers.

The aim is confidence, not technical training. The officers should learn the Society's workflows and the platform's review steps; they should not have to learn the underlying website technology.

### Target state by December 31, 2026

By the end of the calendar year, routine work should follow this pattern:

> Secretary or Assistant Secretary → prepare → review → approve → publish

"Email Edward" should no longer be a normal step. The Secretary and Assistant Secretary should be able to maintain member information, prepare communications, and publish approved material without depending on Edward.

The officers may decide to divide preparation and approval between them—for example, one officer prepares a change and the other gives final approval. Whatever division of responsibility they choose, the platform should support it without adding a permanent technical intermediary.

After the transition, Edward's role should be limited to exceptional maintenance, troubleshooting, or future development. Success means that the Society can continue its ordinary work even when he is unavailable.

## Near-term roadmap

### Complete Create Event

Provide an officer-facing process for creating a new event from a source document. It should guide the officer through document upload, AI-assisted drafting, human review, website preview, approval, and publication without requiring technical intervention.

### Complete Create Call

Provide a similar process for calls for papers, nominations, or other Society submissions. The system should turn an approved source document into a structured draft while preserving human review of dates, requirements, contacts, and wording.

### Remove Edward from routine workflows

Move the final review and publishing controls into the Executive Administration Platform. Officers should be able to complete a safe update themselves, with clear summaries, previews, confirmations, and useful error messages.

### Improve instructions and handoff

Document the small number of routine tasks officers actually perform. Test those instructions with the incoming Secretary and Assistant Secretary, revise them where necessary, and record how responsibilities are divided.

### Preserve safeguards while simplifying the experience

Self-management does not mean one-click publication without review. The finished platform should continue to show proposed changes, check for missing or invalid information, provide previews where appropriate, require deliberate approval, and retain a practical recovery path when something goes wrong.

## What success looks like

The platform will have achieved its purpose when:

- incoming officers can perform ordinary administrative work without programming knowledge;
- SAGP maintains one reliable, approved version of its important information;
- membership, communications, and website publication reinforce one another instead of relying on separate copies;
- AI reduces repetitive data entry while people retain responsibility for accuracy and approval;
- the Secretary and Assistant Secretary can run the routine workflow themselves by the end of 2026; and
- no single person—including Edward—is required for the Society's day-to-day operation.

The immediate goal is therefore not merely to transfer a website. It is to transfer a clear, sustainable way of maintaining and publishing the Society's institutional knowledge.
