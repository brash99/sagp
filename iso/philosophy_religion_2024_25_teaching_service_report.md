## 1. Purpose and evidentiary boundary

This report describes the teaching and service contributions that ISO can observe for the Department of Philosophy and Religion during the complete 2024–25 academic cycle. For this report, that cycle comprises Fall 2024, Spring 2025, and both Summer 2025 terms. It is descriptive, not evaluative.

The report also presents each faculty member's **FY26 beginning and final LHE expectations** and any explicit adjustment or special role recorded in Carolyn Davis's workbook. Those expectations apply to **2025–26**, one academic year after the observed teaching period. They are provided as institutional context only. This report does not divide observed SCH, sections, or courses by expected LHE; does not calculate observed-to-expected ratios; and does not infer under-teaching, over-teaching, productivity, fairness, adequacy, quality, or institutional value.

Four evidence scopes remain distinct:

1. The **departmental instructional portfolio** includes every observed PHIL and RSTD section in the 2024–25 cycle, regardless of the instructor's current workforce status or governed home unit.
2. The **current faculty teaching table** includes instruction attributed through governed identity resolution to the 13 current full-time department faculty.
3. The **governance-service table** records resolved appearances in the official 2024–25 committee roster.
4. The **LHE context table** transcribes institutional expectations and adjustments from the separate FY26 workbook.

Section and SCH totals are cumulative across the selected fall, spring, and summer terms. Independent studies and practica can carry low enrollment or one credit and still count as sections. A governance roster record establishes a published appointment, not attendance, effort, quality, effectiveness, or the absence of other service.

They can reflect adjunct instruction, instruction by faculty outside the current cohort, or instruction by faculty whose governed home unit lies elsewhere. The report does not silently reassign those sections.

## 7. Overall characterization

The 2024–25 evidence depicts Philosophy and Religion as a two-prefix instructional department whose current full-time faculty collectively teach both philosophical and religious-studies curricula. The complete PHIL/RSTD portfolio contains 90 sections and 5,588 SCH. Within the current cohort, PHIL instruction accounts for 37 sections and 2,295 SCH, while RSTD instruction accounts for 33 sections and 1,629 SCH. The course record includes general and LLC instruction, upper-level disciplinary courses, writing-intensive work, seminars, practica, and independent studies.

The department also participates in institution-wide instruction through Honors, IDST, and Environmental Studies. The governance evidence records committee relationships involving curriculum, assessment, faculty review, teaching support, university chairs, athletics, Honors, Phi Beta Kappa, Faculty Senate, and pre-law advising. Carolyn's separate FY26 workbook adds institutional context by identifying assigned LHE expectations and explicit roles or adjustments, without converting those facts into an evaluative measure.

These observations establish the existence and measured extent of particular contributions. They do not make the contributions commensurable, establish sufficiency, assign weights, or determine institutional materiality.

## 9. Limitations and non-meanings

- Schedule observations establish instructional delivery, not teaching quality or effectiveness.
- SCH is an instructional-volume measure, not a measure of faculty quality, course importance, curricular necessity, or institutional value.
- Section counts include different course types, credit values, enrollments, and instructional forms. A section is not a standardized workload unit.
- The current-cohort table does not attribute every PHIL/RSTD section to the 13 current faculty.
- Governance evidence is limited to the official 2024–25 roster represented in ISO. It does not comprehensively observe department, college, professional, community, advising, mentoring, or informal service.
- Roster-record counts are provenance counts, not workload measures.
- FY26 LHE expectations are time-bounded institutional assignments. They are not timeless faculty attributes.
- The report does not compare observed teaching in 2024–25 with expected LHE in 2025–26.
- No observed/expected ratio, normalized measure, scalar contribution score, ranking, recommendation, or personnel evaluation is produced.
