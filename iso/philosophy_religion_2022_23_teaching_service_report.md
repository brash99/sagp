## 1. Purpose and evidentiary boundary

This report describes the teaching and service contributions that ISO can observe for the Department of Philosophy and Religion during the complete 2022–23 academic cycle. It is descriptive, not evaluative.

The report also presents each faculty member's **FY26 beginning and final LHE expectations** and explicit adjustment or special role recorded in Carolyn Davis's workbook. Those expectations apply to **2025–26**, three academic years after the observed teaching period. They are later institutional context only. This report does not back-project them onto 2022–23, divide observed teaching by expected LHE, calculate observed-to-expected ratios, or infer workload adequacy, productivity, fairness, quality, or institutional value.

Three evidence scopes remain distinct:

1. The **departmental instructional portfolio** includes every distinct observed PHIL and RSTD section in the 2022–23 cycle, regardless of the instructor's current workforce status or governed home unit.
2. The **current faculty teaching table** includes instruction attributed through governed identity resolution to the 13 current full-time department faculty.
3. The **LHE context table** transcribes later institutional expectations and adjustments from the FY26 workbook.

No authoritative 2022–23 Academic Standing Committees workbook or governed 2022–23 appointment registry is present in the repository. Accordingly, this report describes observed cross-unit service teaching but does not substitute a later committee roster for missing same-year governance evidence.

Section and SCH totals are cumulative across the selected fall, spring, May, and summer terms. PHIL/RSTD offerings occur in Fall 2022, Spring 2023, May Term 2023, Summer Term I 2023, and Extended Summer 2023; no PHIL/RSTD offering is observed in Summer Term II 2023. The source schedule contains nine duplicated Fall 2022 PHIL/RSTD rows. Portfolio totals use distinct governed course-offering identities (term plus CRN), not raw row count. Independent studies and practica can carry low enrollment or one credit and still count as sections.

## 2. Department overview

The governed department structure identifies Philosophy and Religion as the owner of the **PHIL** and **RSTD** instructional prefixes. Across the complete 2022–23 instructional cycle, the observed PHIL/RSTD portfolio contains **110 distinct sections, 1,848 enrollments, and 5,534 SCH**.

The 13 current full-time department faculty account for **72 of those PHIL/RSTD sections and 3,437 SCH**. Their department-owned instruction divides into **43 PHIL sections (2,145 SCH)** and **29 RSTD sections (1,292 SCH)**. They also taught **six sections (339 SCH)** outside PHIL and RSTD: five HONR sections and one EVST section.

The difference between the complete department portfolio and the current-cohort table is **38 PHIL/RSTD sections and 2,097 SCH**. Those observations are parts of the department's instructional portfolio but are not attributed here to the current 13-person full-time cohort. They can reflect adjunct instruction, instruction by faculty outside the current cohort, former faculty, or faculty whose governed home unit lies elsewhere. Vered Sakal has no schedule observation in the selected period; this is reported as an evidence observation, not an inference about employment or contribution.

- SCH is an instructional-volume measure, not a measure of faculty quality, course importance, curricular necessity, or institutional value.
- Section counts include different course types, credit values, enrollments, and instructional forms.
- The current-cohort table does not attribute every PHIL/RSTD section to the 13 current faculty.
- Current 2026 workforce membership does not establish membership or employment status in 2022–23.
- No authoritative same-year governance roster is available; no later roster is substituted.
- FY26 LHE expectations are not evidence of 2022–23 expectations.
- No observed/expected ratio, normalized measure, scalar contribution score, ranking, recommendation, or personnel evaluation is produced.
