## 1. Purpose and evidentiary boundary

This report combines ISO's four annual Philosophy and Religion teaching-and-service reports into a longitudinal description of observed departmental instruction, current-cohort instruction, cross-unit service teaching, and documented governance service. It is descriptive, not evaluative.

The four-year total is a cumulative observation, not a normalized faculty measure. It does not adjust for differing appointments, leaves, sabbaticals, administrative roles, employment periods, course forms, credits, enrollments, or evidence availability. Current 2026 workforce membership does not establish that every person belonged to the workforce in every earlier year.

The report preserves four distinct evidence scopes:

1. The **complete departmental portfolio** includes every distinct observed PHIL and RSTD section, regardless of instructor status or governed home unit.
2. The **current-cohort tables** include instruction attributed through governed identity resolution to the 13 current full-time department faculty.
3. The **governance account** reports only appointments supported by an authoritative roster for that academic year.
4. The **FY26 LHE table** reports institutionally assigned teaching expectations for 2025–26 only. It is not back-projected or used to normalize any teaching observation.

## 2. Four-year department overview

Across the four complete academic cycles, the observed PHIL/RSTD portfolio contains **390 distinct sections, 7,678 enrollments, and 22,988 SCH**. PHIL accounts for **218 sections and 14,097 SCH**; RSTD accounts for **172 sections and 8,891 SCH**.

The current 13-person cohort accounts for **286 PHIL/RSTD sections and 16,008 SCH**: **155 PHIL sections and 9,313 SCH**, plus **131 RSTD sections and 6,695 SCH**. The cohort also delivered **28 sections and 1,377 SCH** outside PHIL and RSTD, producing **314 total observed sections and 17,385 SCH**.

The complete portfolio exceeds the current-cohort PHIL/RSTD observations by **104 sections and 6,980 SCH**. That difference can include adjunct instruction, former faculty, faculty outside the current cohort, or faculty whose governed home unit lies elsewhere. It must not be interpreted as missing work by current faculty.

The two observed years document appointments involving university chairs, Honors, Phi Beta Kappa, student-survey support, the library, pre-law advising, assessment, Faculty Senate, curriculum review, faculty review, athletics, Liberal Learning, effective teaching, academic program review, and faculty grievance and hearing. These records establish published relationships, not attendance, effort, effectiveness, relative importance, or the absence of other service.

No four-year governance total is assigned to individual faculty because the first two years lack comparable authoritative rosters. Summing only the observed years without that qualification would create a misleading longitudinal measure.

## 6. FY26 teaching expectations and special-role context

Carolyn Davis's workbook provides authoritative teaching-responsibility context for **2025–26 only**. For the 13-person cohort it records **276 beginning LHE**, **66 LHE of typed adjustments**, and **210 final expected LHE**.

The recorded adjustments include 36 research LHE, 12 administrative LHE, and 18 sabbatical LHE. Explicit contexts include Betsy Jelinek's department-chair role, Vered Sakal's Jewish Studies program-director role, John Thompson's Spring FY26 sabbatical, and Stephen Strehle's Fall FY26 sabbatical.

These facts help describe FY26 institutional assignments. They do not establish expectations for 2022–23, 2023–24, or 2024–25 and are not used to create an observed/expected ratio, normalize the four-year record, or explain earlier annual variation.

## 7. Longitudinal observations

The evidence supports several bounded descriptive observations:

- The complete department-owned portfolio remains substantial in every observed year, ranging from 89 to 110 distinct sections and from 5,534 to 6,011 SCH.
- Both PHIL and RSTD contribute materially to the observed portfolio in every year. The balance changes across years, but the data alone do not establish why.
- Current department faculty repeatedly deliver instruction outside PHIL and RSTD through Honors, Environmental Studies, and interdisciplinary courses.
- The observed record combines high-enrollment courses with seminars, practica, independent studies, and other low-enrollment forms. SCH and section counts therefore expose different aspects of instructional activity.
- Governance evidence demonstrates institutional service in the two years for which authoritative rosters are available, while the first two years remain an explicit evidence gap.
- The present evidence does not make teaching, governance, research, advising, leadership, and other contributions commensurable.

## 8. Annual-window reconciliation

The four reports do not all use an identical summer-term pattern. The 2022–23, 2023–24, and 2025–26 reports include their explicitly listed May and extended-summer terms. The 2024–25 report defines its governed observation window as Fall 2024, Spring 2025, Summer Term 1 2025, and Summer Term 2 2025; it does not include May or Extended Summer 2025.

This summary follows those published annual definitions rather than silently broadening 2024–25. When the exact annual term sets are applied, the current governed faculty contribution objects reproduce the annual current-cohort tables and the four-year total of **314 sections and 17,385 SCH**. This explicit alignment is necessary for reproducibility.

## 10. Limitations and non-meanings

- Schedule observations establish instructional delivery, not teaching quality or effectiveness.
- SCH is an instructional-volume measure, not a measure of faculty quality, curricular necessity, or institutional value.
- Section counts combine course forms with different credits, enrollments, pedagogies, and purposes.
- The current cohort is defined using the August 11, 2026 workforce and is not a historical workforce census for every annual period.
- Absence of a schedule observation is not evidence of absence of employment, assigned responsibility, or contribution.
- Governance evidence is incomplete for the first two years and cannot support a comparable four-year individual service total.
- FY26 teaching expectations apply only to FY26 and cannot normalize earlier observations.
- Annual observation windows differ in their explicitly included summer terms; comparisons must retain those definitions.
- No scalar contribution score, ranking, recommendation, personnel evaluation, or institutional decision is produced.
